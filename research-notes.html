Working hypothesis · 13 November 2026

At the family researcher’s request, the atlas tentatively connects David Salomon (“Jud David”) → Kaufmann David → David Kaufmann (later Baum) → Isaak Baum. The first two parent–child links are tentative. Isaak’s death register directly names David Baum as his father; equating that David with David Kaufmann remains a working identification. Kaufmann is standardized with one f; Kauffmann is preserved as a recorded spelling. The proposed reading treats Kaufmann David as Kaufmann, son of David, and David Kaufmann as David, son of Kaufmann, rather than assuming a modern hereditary surname at both generations. The 1799 wording itself admits uncertainty.

Kaiser’s Wittelsberg chronicle · p. 141

The local transcription of IMG_0008.jpg says that in 1748 ten Jews lived in Wittelsberg, and “Jud David” was a cattle dealer owning a simple house. ## Josbach: Auguste Steinfeld's family

- Schneider p. 269: Auguste Steinfeld was born 26 November 1857 and married Jakob Bachrach on 7 October 1884. He gives Jakob's parents as Noah Bachrach and Hennel Spier, and identifies Auguste's parents as Meier Steinfeld and Merle Haas.
- Meier Steinfeld and Merle Haas married 28 August 1847. Schneider gives his birth year as 1818 and death as 9 May 1898; the 2017 report gives birth as 1819. Merle was born 27 December 1821 and died 15 April 1878. (pp. 269, 292.)
- Their home was **Josbach house no. 19**. Schneider's fire-insurance register describes a two-story residence, 9.6 × 8.7 m, an addition 2.3 × 6.1 m, and a two-story barn 3.3 × 8.6 m. The record lists their son Herz and wife Hannchen there in 1885, and Abraham Steinfeld and Paula Katten in 1923. Historical house no. 19 has not been matched to a current street address. (p. 274.)
- The insurance register names Meier Steinfeld and five others as each holding one-sixth of Josbach synagogue no. 83, described as 7.5 × 5.8 m with an altar, 15 benches, a gallery and a cupboard. (p. 275.)
- Schneider p. 268 identifies Meier's parents as Salomon (Israel) Steinfeld (born 1793, died 26 April 1848) and Röschen Stern (born 1789, died 18 April 1868). He names Salomon's parents as Hirsch/Herz Meyer and Lea, daughter of Salm Israel; Röschen's as Hirsch Stern and Frommet, née Jakob, of Gilserberg.
- Schneider pp. 275–276 transcribes Salomon Steinfeld and Röschen Stern's civil marriage record, 22 October 1811. Salomon was described as an 18-year-old merchant; his father Hirsch Meyer had died in Schiffelbach on 6 January 1794. Salomon's mother Lea consented. Röschen was described as 22; her mother Frommet Jakob had died 24 August 1811. The record describes the reading of provisions from the Napoleonic code and includes witnesses. Schneider cites `StA Marburg, Protokolle II/4 Josbach 1811`; obtain and compare the original. Schneider titles the transcription a marriage contract, but its text is a civil marriage record with related banns.

## Schneider: historical context and further details

- **Lea's shop (p. 266):** Schneider says Lea, the widow of Hirsch Meyer and mother of Salomon Steinfeld, probably moved from Schiffelbach to Josbach after Hirsch's death in 1794. She ran a small trade in barley, rice, flour, oil and spices; after her death in 1822 Salomon continued it. Schneider cites StA Marburg, Bestand 19 h, Nr. 928. Her movement is Schneider's inference, whereas the shop is reported from the archival material.
- **The Steinfeld family in the 1858 survey (pp. 266–267):** An official local report dated 25 September 1858 describes Meier Steinfeld, 40, as a wholesale merchant with sufficient means, listing Adelheid, age 2, and Guste, three-quarters of a year. Guste is plausibly Auguste, born 26 November 1857, given the parent and approximate age. The same survey lists a Salomon aged 10, but Schneider p. 269 lists Meier and Merle's Salomon as born 9 June 1848 and dead 12 August 1848. This internal contradiction needs an original-record check; do not assume the 1858 Salomon was the child who died in 1848.
- **Josbach worship and burial (p. 267):** Josbach, Halsdorf and Wohra shared the Halsdorf synagogue community, school and ritual bath. In 1878 a small Josbach prayer house, house no. 83, was founded by six community members, including Meier and his brothers Levi and Liebmann Steinfeld, each with a one-sixth share. It measured 7.5 × 5.8 m and had 15 benches and a gallery. It passed to non-Jewish ownership in October 1938 before the November pogrom, was later used as a barn and demolished. Josbach Jewish burials went to Hatzbach until the Halsdorf cemetery opened in 1903. Distinguish No. 83 (prayer house) from Meier and Merle's home No. 19.
- **Josbach setting (p. 265):** Predominantly agricultural, with a few crafts and two mills. Its position on the Frankfurt–Kassel long-distance road brought two inns and a post station. Schneider gives 38 Jewish inhabitants among 348 total in 1861.
- **Mardorf and the Haas family (pp. 279, 282):** The village was mainly Catholic and remained under the archbishopric of Mainz until 1802/03. A report for 1823 identifies Samuel Haas as a merchant, assisted by his son Emanuel, while his son Simon was learning shoemaking. In 1858 his son Mendel Haas was described as trading 170–180 cattle annually, taking around 5–6 to each market, and also slaughtering on festive occasions. These are Schneider's abstracts of historical reports, not estimates independently checked here.
- **Mardorf protection rights and the David Baruch identification (pp. 281–282):** Schneider says a man recorded first as Baruch/Barach and later as David Baruch obtained protection in Mardorf in 1714; payments were ten guilders per year through 1729 and three from 1730. Schneider states he died in the third quarter of 1751. He identifies later Haas, Lion, Maas and Kaiser families as descendants, but the proposed identification with the other report's Baruch ben David still needs primary-source checking. If it is the same person, the 2008 report's estimated birth about 1715 cannot be right if he held protection in 1714.
- **A dispute at worship (p. 283):** Schneider describes a summer 1781 court dispute in which Mendel Baruch's wife complained that Löb Baruch's wife struck her in the face during a service. A witness said the quarrel occurred as the Torah or Ten Commandments were being taken out. Schneider's chart suggests the men were brothers, but his genealogy also reports Mendel dead by 1774/75; avoid asserting the woman's name or precise status without checking the source (Stiftsarchiv Amöneburg, Kommissariats-Protokolle 1773–1781, pp. 169 ff.).
- **Wittelsberg schooling context (p. 284):** In 1852 teacher Hirsch Hahn taught 27 children across Mardorf, Roßdorf and Rauischholzhausen, including Wittelsberg. Schneider describes changes in the shared arrangements in 1858 and after Hahn died in 1870. This is community context; we have not identified an individual ancestor in the class list.
- **Relatives who remained in Josbach (pp. 267, 269):** Meier and Merle's son Herz Steinfeld and his wife Hannchen lived in house no. 19; later occupants included their son Abraham and his wife Paula, née Katten. Abraham died in 1929. Schneider says Paula and their daughter Irma were deported to the Riga ghetto in December 1941 and died in Stutthof; he gives Paula's death as 30 December 1944. Abraham and Paula's son Manfred emigrated to the United States in 1938 and, in 1992, had a memorial placed for his mother and sister at the Halsdorf cemetery. Manfred and Irma were Auguste Steinfeld's great-nephew and great-niece. These are Schneider's reported dates and identifications; verify against the underlying records when needed.

## Paula Baum Collection, reel 1: manual document readings, 13 November 2026

Source: local `Sources/Paula Baum Collection/Paula Baum Collection - reel 1.pdf`. **Page references in this section are 1-based PDF pages, not necessarily the photographed frame number.** Read from enlarged page images; existing OCR and the old collection inventory are finding aids, not authorities for the readings. Square brackets identify uncertain readings or unread text. Translations below are working translations, not certified diplomatic transcriptions. A partial translation is explicitly labeled; an unread passage must not be treated as evidence that it contains no genealogical information.

The David Kaufmann/David Baum in the eighteenth- and early nineteenth-century documents is the earlier Wittelsberg man associated with Röschen and Isaac, not Paula's husband David Baum (born 1869). Identifications with the established family remain separate from relationships explicitly stated in each document.

### PDF pp. 251–252: order concerning David Kaufmann's grain and fodder purchases, 7 December 1792

**Correction to the earlier description:** the old inventory calls this a licence to trade in grain/fodder. The document itself **prohibits purchases contrary to a government ordinance and demands an accounting and proof of supplier status**. It is not an issued trading licence.

Working transcription of the operative passage:

> Nachdem dem Amte [soeben?] hinterbracht worden, daß der Jud David Kauffmann von Wittelsberg sich [beikommen?] lasse, Frucht und Fourage gegen Hochfürstl. Regierungs Verordnung, welche bereits in dem ganzen Gericht Wittelsberg [längstens?] bekannt gemacht worden, anzukaufen, als wird demselben ein solches hierdurch nochmahlen bei alsbaldiger Strafe von [2 Rthl.?] nicht nur verboten, sondern es hat auch derselbe künftigen Montag den 10t d. M. ein genaues Verzeichnis aller angekauften Früchte und Fourage […] an mich einzuliefern, und darinnen sich zugleich hinlänglich zu rechtfertigen, für wen er diesen Frucht und Fouragehandel bisher getrieben; […] durch einen erforderlichen Schein des sich jetzt in Marburg aufhaltenden hochfürstl. Hessischen Herrn Kriegs-Rath [Engelhard?] als Lieferant für die hochfürstlich hessischen Truppen zu legitimieren.

**Translation:**

> Since it has been reported to this office that the Jew David Kauffmann of Wittelsberg has been purchasing grain and fodder contrary to the princely government's ordinance, which has already been announced throughout the court district of Wittelsberg, he is hereby once again forbidden to do so, under penalty of an immediate fine of [two Reichsthaler?]. He must also submit to me, by next Monday, the tenth of this month, an exact list of all the grain and fodder he has purchased, [in a form that he can substantiate on oath; wording partly unclear]. In it he must also satisfactorily explain for whom he has hitherto conducted this grain and fodder trade. Within the same prescribed period, and under the same penalty, he must establish his status as a supplier to the princely Hessian troops by producing the required certificate from the princely Hessian war councillor [Engelhard?], who is currently in Marburg.
>
> [Place/signature not securely read], 7 December 1792.

Here *Frucht* means grain, not modern fruit; *Fourage* means fodder. The reverse, p. 252, bears a short forwarding/notification instruction ending **“zur alsbaldigen Bekanntmachung”** (“for immediate notification”); the recipient's designation is not securely read.

**Relevance:** direct evidence of David's grain/fodder business and official scrutiny. The order requires proof of a military-supply arrangement; it does **not** by itself prove that he held the contract, complied with the order, paid a fine, or was convicted.

### PDF p. 253: Boila/Bela Kaufmann purchases household goods from her mother's property, 1802

**Status: heading and receipt translated; itemization only partly deciphered.**

Heading:

> Extract. derjenigen Mobilien welche die Jüdin Boila Kauffmann[in] aus dem Mobilien Vermögen ihrer Mutter zu Wittelsberg erkauft.

**Translation:**

> Extract [listing] the movable household goods which the Jewish woman Boila Kauffmann purchased from her mother's movable property at Wittelsberg.

The concluding receipt reads, in part:

> Vorstehender Betrag ist mit 9 fl. 25 alb. 4 hl. von der Boila Kauffmann[in] bezahlt […] [15 June; year 1802 on the archival date tab].

**Translation:**
